### Clause Definition: Definition of Deficiency
**Summary:** Defines "Deficiency" as any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in performance required by law or contract, including negligence, omission, commission, or deliberate withholding of information that causes loss or injury to the consumer.

**Verbatim Regulatory Text:**
> "Deficiency" signifies any fault, imperfection, shortcoming, or inadequacy in the quality, nature, and manner of performance which is required to be maintained by or under any law for the time being in force, or has been undertaken to be performed by a person in pursuance of a contract or otherwise in relation to any service. The legal definition explicitly encompasses: - Any act of negligence or omission or commission by such person which causes loss or injury to the consumer. - The deliberate withholding of relevant information by such person to the consumer.

### Clause Section 2: Definition of Unfair Trade Practice and Covered Actions
**Summary:** Defines "unfair trade practice" and enumerates specific actions that constitute such practices, including false statements, misrepresentation of goods, deceptive warranties, misleading advertisements, spurious goods, and refusal to refund defective goods within the stipulated time period.

**Verbatim Regulatory Text:**
> An "unfair trade practice" is a trade practice which, for the purpose of promoting the sale, use, or supply of any goods or for the provision of any service, adopts any unfair method or unfair or deceptive practice. Covered Actions: This broadly encompasses, but is not limited to, the following practices: - Making false or misleading statements regarding a product's or service's standard, quality, quantity, or grade. - Falsely representing re-built, second-hand, renovated, or old goods as new goods. - Giving the public any warranty or guarantee of the performance, efficacy, or length of life of a product that is not based on an adequate or proper test. - Publishing deceptive bargain advertisements or permitting the offering of gifts and prizes with the intention of not providing them as offered. - Manufacturing or offering spurious goods for sale, or hoarding/destroying goods to artificially raise their cost. - Refusing to withdraw defective goods or deficient services and refund the consideration within the stipulated time period.

### Clause Section 46: Jurisdiction and applicability of Indian law to policies issued in India
**Summary:** Holders of policies issued in India have the right to receive payment in India and to sue in Indian courts irrespective of policy terms; Indian law governs all questions of law arising from such policies.

**Exceptions & Overrides:**
- Provided that nothing in this section shall apply to a policy of marine insurance.

**Verbatim Regulatory Text:**
> The holder of a policy of insurance issued by an insurer in respect of insurance business transacted in India after the commencement of this Act shall have the right, notwithstanding anything to the contrary contained in the policy or in any agreement relating thereto, to receive payment in India, of any sum secured thereby and to sue for any relief in respect of the policy in any court of competent jurisdiction in India; and if the suit is brought in India any question of law arising in connection with any such policy shall be determined according to the law in force in India.

### Clause Section 64VB(1): Risk assumption contingent on premium receipt
**Summary:** An insurer may not assume any risk in India unless the premium is received in advance or guaranteed to be paid as prescribed.

**Verbatim Regulatory Text:**
> No insurer shall assume any risk in India in respect of any insurance business on which premium is not ordinarily payable outside India unless and until the premium payable is received by him or is guaranteed to be paid by such person in such manner and within such time as may be prescribed or unless and until deposit of such amount as may be prescribed, is made in advance in the prescribed manner.

### Clause Section 64VB(2): Earliest risk assumption date upon premium payment
**Summary:** For risks where premium can be ascertained in advance, the insurer may assume risk no earlier than the date the premium is paid in cash or by cheque.

**Verbatim Regulatory Text:**
> For the purposes of this section, in the case of risks for which premium can be ascertained in advance, the risk may be assumed not earlier than the date on which the premium has been paid in cash or by cheque to the insurer. Explanation.—Where the premium is tendered by postal money order or cheque sent by post, the risk may be assumed on the date on which the money order is booked or the cheque is posted, as the case may be.
