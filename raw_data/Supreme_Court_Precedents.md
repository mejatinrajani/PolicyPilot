# Supreme Court of India - Landmark Insurance Precedents

## 1. Delayed Intimation of Claim
**Case Title:** Om Prakash vs. Reliance General Insurance and Anr.
**Citation:** (2017) 9 SCC 724
**Core Legal Principle:** The Supreme Court ruled that a genuine claim cannot be rejected solely on the hyper-technical ground of delayed intimation if the substantive facts of the incident (e.g., theft or accident) are proven and an FIR was filed. The Consumer Protection Act is a beneficial legislation that deserves liberal construction.
**LLM Trigger:** Invoke this precedent IF the insurer's rejection reason is "Delay in Intimation," "Late Filing of Claim," or failure to inform the insurer within 24/48 hours.

## 2. Irrelevant Non-Disclosure (Pre-Existing Diseases)
**Case Title:** Manmohan Nanda vs. United India Assurance Co. Ltd.
**Citation:** (2022) 4 SCC 582
**Core Legal Principle:** The Supreme Court held that an insurer cannot repudiate a claim for non-disclosure if the alleged pre-existing medical condition had no causal connection to the actual illness or condition for which the policyholder was hospitalized. The insurer must prove a direct, material link.
**LLM Trigger:** Invoke this precedent IF the insurer's rejection reason is "Non-Disclosure of Material Facts" AND the hidden medical condition is unrelated to the current hospitalization cause.

## 3. Hidden or Uncommunicated Exclusion Clauses
**Case Title:** M/S Texco Marketing Pvt. Ltd. vs. TATA AIG General Insurance Co. Ltd.
**Citation:** (2022 INSC 1184)
**Core Legal Principle:** The Court applied the "Main Purpose Rule," stating that wide exclusion clauses cannot override the main object of the insurance policy. If the insurer fails to explicitly bring an exclusion clause to the knowledge of the insured at the time of policy issuance, they cannot rely on it to deny a claim.
**LLM Trigger:** Invoke this precedent IF the insurer rejects a claim based on a specific "Exclusion Clause" that the user claims was buried in fine print, not communicated, or completely contradicts the primary purpose of the policy.

## 4. Ambiguity in Policy Wording (Contra Proferentem)
**Case Title:** Sushilaben Indravadan Gandhi vs. New India Assurance Co. Ltd.
**Citation:** (2020 INSC)
**Core Legal Principle:** The Supreme Court reaffirmed the Doctrine of Contra Proferentem. If there is any ambiguity, vagueness, or dual interpretation in the wording of the insurance contract, the interpretation that favors the policyholder (the party who did not draft the contract) must strictly be applied.
**LLM Trigger:** Invoke this precedent IF the insurer's rejection relies on a vague, undefined term in the policy schedule, or if the insurer and policyholder disagree on the definition of a specific word used in the contract.
